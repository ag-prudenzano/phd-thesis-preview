## Research Question

Can speaking in Discord voice channels support offline social confidence and engagement among adults with elevated social anxiety?

My PhD research examines whether speaking in Discord voice channels can support offline social confidence and engagement among adults with elevated social anxiety.

## Why Discord Voice?

Discord voice occupies an unusual space between text-based communication and face-to-face interaction. Conversations are live, spontaneous, and socially demanding, yet users retain forms of control that are rarely available offline: they can choose a familiar community, mute themselves, enter or leave a channel, and participate without being physically visible. This combination may make voice channels approachable enough for socially anxious users while still providing meaningful experience of real-time conversation.

## Central Model

The thesis investigates whether this experience can function as a bridge to offline socialising. Its central model proposes that speaking in Discord voice channels may strengthen offline social self-efficacy—the belief that one can initiate, join, and manage face-to-face interactions—which may subsequently encourage more voluntary offline social engagement.

However, online participation is not assumed to be inherently beneficial or harmful. The research distinguishes four possible patterns:

### Bridge

Experience gained through Discord voice supports confidence and participation offline.

### Non-Transfer

Voice participation is socially meaningful, but its effects remain within the online setting.

### Substitution

Discord becomes a safer alternative that reduces motivation to approach offline interaction.

### Compensation

Discord provides valuable connection when offline opportunities are limited, without necessarily displacing them.

## Opportunity and Behaviour

This distinction matters because low offline engagement does not always indicate avoidance. Someone may have the confidence and motivation to socialise but lack accessible opportunities. By separating opportunity from behaviour, the thesis aims to produce a more precise account of what online social participation can—and cannot—change.

## Three-Study Design

The project uses a three-study mixed-methods design.

### Study 1

Study 1 develops and refines the central concepts and measures through qualitative interviews and pilot testing.

### Study 2

Study 2 tests the proposed pathway prospectively through a four-wave longitudinal survey.

### Study 3

Study 3 returns to a purposive subsample for qualitative follow-up interviews, exploring how participants understand trajectories of transfer, non-transfer, substitution, and compensation in their own lives.

## Contribution

Rather than treating “screen time” or “social media use” as uniform experiences, this research focuses on a specific behaviour with specific interpersonal demands: actively speaking in live voice channels. In doing so, it asks a more useful question than whether online communication is simply good or bad: for whom, under what conditions, and through which mechanisms might online interaction shape life offline?
